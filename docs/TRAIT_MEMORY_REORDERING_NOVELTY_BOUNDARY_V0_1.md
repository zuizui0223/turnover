# Novelty boundary: global phylogenetic-memory spectrum versus lineage re-ranking

Status: literature-positioning note for the BIEN discovery + prospectively frozen AusTraits replication.

## What is already known

### Phylogenetic signal is widespread, and trait categories differ on average

Blomberg, Garland & Ives (2003, Evolution; DOI 10.1111/j.0014-3820.2003.tb00285.x) established that phylogenetic signal is common and reported weaker average signal in behavioural traits than in body-size, morphometric and several other classes.

This already supports the idea of a **global trait/category tendency**. We therefore must not claim to discover that different traits or trait classes differ in phylogenetic structure.

### The same trait can behave differently in different clades

Ackerly (2009, PNAS; DOI 10.1073/pnas.0901635106) showed large among-clade differences in evolutionary rates for the same functional traits, including plant height and leaf size.

Ives, Midford & Garland / Zheng et al. (Functional Ecology 2010; DOI 10.1111/j.1365-2435.2009.01596.x) explicitly discuss plant examples where apparent phylogenetic signal in a broad taxonomic sample weakens or disappears within a restricted clade, and develop multivariate signal tests that incorporate trait correlations and measurement error.

Therefore neither **clade dependence**, **heterogeneous phylogenetic signal**, nor **correlated traits** is itself novel.

### Phylogenetic signal is not an evolutionary-rate estimator or a mechanism

Revell, Harmon & Collar (2008, Systematic Biology; DOI 10.1080/10635150802302427) and Ackerly (2009) make clear that signal and evolutionary rate/process are not interchangeable.

Westoby et al. (2023, Journal of Ecology; DOI 10.1111/1365-2745.14150) further emphasizes that phylogenetic pattern can combine historical inheritance with continuing ecological/functional constraints.

The present study must therefore describe rho as a **phylogenetic-memory statistic**, not a direct rate of evolution or a diagnosed causal mechanism.

## The unresolved question

Existing work establishes two facts that are usually discussed separately:

1. traits differ in average phylogenetic signal/conservatism;
2. signal and evolutionary dynamics vary among clades.

Those facts do not tell us whether the **relative ordering of traits** is itself transferable.

The sharper question is:

> If trait A is more phylogenetically conservative than trait B in the global data, how often is A still more conservative than B inside a previously unseen lineage?

This is a prediction/portability question rather than another test for the existence of phylogenetic signal.

## What the current programme adds

The crossed family x trait design separates three levels that a pooled comparison can conflate:

- **global trait spectrum**: average differences among traits;
- **lineage-wide context**: family-level shifts shared across represented traits;
- **lineage-specific re-ranking**: family x trait deviations that can reverse the relative order of two traits.

The BIEN discovery shows the nontrivial conjunction:

1. a reproducible global trait spectrum exists;
2. trait identity carries modest out-of-family predictive information on the cleaned log scale;
3. nevertheless, the same two traits reverse relative memory ordering between families at approximately 46-48% frequency for well-represented pairs;
4. a measurement-error-aware latent decomposition predicts essentially the same reversal frequency.

Thus the novelty is not "phylogenetic signal depends on lineage."

It is the distinction between a **real aggregate hierarchy** and a **portable within-lineage hierarchy**.

## Safest novelty statement

> Global differences among traits in phylogenetic memory can be statistically reproducible without defining a stable conservative-to-labile hierarchy within individual lineages.

Stronger wording, conditional on prospective AusTraits replication:

> A reproducible global spectrum of phylogenetic trait memory coexists with extensive cross-lineage re-ordering, showing that a macroevolutionary regularity can be a useful population-level prior without operating as a conserved lineage-level rule.

## Wording to avoid

- "We show for the first time that phylogenetic signal is lineage dependent."
- "Traits have no intrinsic phylogenetic signal."
- "Phylogenetic memory is determined by lineage rather than trait."
- "The lineage-specific component is an evolutionary rate."
- "Near-half reversal means rankings are random."

## Literature gap that still merits a formal systematic check

A targeted search did not identify a prior study that simultaneously:
- measures the same multiple traits across many independent lineages;
- treats lineage and trait as crossed units;
- evaluates held-out-lineage portability of a trait's relative conservatism rank; and
- reports cross-lineage pair-order reversal as an effect size.

Before journal submission, this should be converted from a targeted novelty audit to a documented systematic search rather than a categorical priority claim.
