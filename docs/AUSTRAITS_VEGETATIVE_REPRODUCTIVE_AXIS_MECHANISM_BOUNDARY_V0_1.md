# Vegetative–reproductive allocation axis: ecological mechanism boundary

Status: post-outcome hypothesis audit, 2026-10-08. The rho results, retrospective factor selection and source-dependent validation decisions are not changed here.

## Observed pattern: what it is and is not

On the fixed 254-system matched-log AusTraits core, ridge-regularized family-by-trait rank-1 extension improved held-out-cell SSE by about 3.2% on S3 and 7.7% on prune relative to an additive family+trait baseline. The factor's loadings are highly concordant between tree treatments.

Negative factor end: petiole length, leaf width, leaf length, leaf area.
Positive factor end: seed length, fruit length, seed dry mass, seed width, fruit width.

This is a contrast in **family-specific values of the distance–trait-dissimilarity rho statistic**, not a measured trade-off between leaf biomass and reproductive investment.

A positive or negative loading is not a negative phenotypic correlation between leaf and seed size. Even if the factor is stable between trees, it does not establish a developmental constraint, ecological mechanism, or adaptive trade-off.

## Literature precedents that constrain novelty

**Ackerly (2009), PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.0901635106**
Compared plant height, leaf size and seed size across four to six woody clades. Found strong differences in directly estimated evolutionary rates across clades, and explicitly distinguished rates from phylogenetic signal. Therefore among-clade differences in the relative evolutionary dynamics of vegetative and reproductive traits are not unprecedented.

**Leaf mass per area macroevolution, 2014, PMC4130440**
Large-scale analysis found low but significant overall LMA conservatism and higher conservatism in woody plants than herbs. Thus growth-form-dependent trait signal and different clade optima are known precedents.

**Seed–phytomer–leaf theory, 2017, PMC5714152**
Links leaf size to seed size, meristem architecture, height and growth strategies in a broad functional context. This is precedent for coordinated vegetative–reproductive strategies, not evidence that the same trade-off drives differences in phylogenetic signal.

**Clade-level leaf–height–seed (LHS) strategy, 2024, PMID 39554526**
Across palms and other major plant groups, the contributions of leaf size and height to seed mass differ by clade. This is precedent for lineage-dependent LHS relationships, although it does not quantify within-family ranking of phylogenetic signal.

## Falsifiable biological hypotheses (not outcomes)

H1: The AusTraits rank-1 factor reflects repeated shifts between a lineage's macroevolutionary constraint on vegetative dimension traits and its constraint on reproductive-size traits.

H2: The factor is generated by the source's dense and internally correlated leaf/fruit/seed **measurement set** rather than a general developmental process.

H3: Tree geometry and species state distributions induce shared changes in rho among dimensions of the same organ.

## Necessary tests before causal language

1. Re-run a standard-signal statistic, Blomberg's K, on the identical matched 254-system AusTraits core. If the organ contrast disappears, its ecological interpretation as allocation of phylogenetic conservatism fails.
2. Within overlapping species and branches, evaluate multivariate evolutionary-rate or phylogenetic covariance models of logarithmic vegetative and reproductive measurements. A contrast in rho alone cannot diagnose evolutionary rate, constraint, or a negative genetic/phenotypic trade-off.
3. Compare species-state covariance and actual evolutionary covariance across the leaf and seed dimensions, rather than inferring them from rho factor loading signs.
4. Test in an independently chosen trait universe: current BIEN data show essentially no low-rank CV gain. The nonreplication is itself a substantial caveat.

## Main manuscript implication

Until this is tested, describe the AusTraits axis as a **dataset-specific statistical signal-allocation pattern**.

Do not call it a universal vegetative–reproductive trade-off, developmental suppression/reactivation, adaptive allocation, or conserved trait module. Those claims concern evolutionary process and biological mechanism, neither identified by the present statistic.

The current generalizable result, if any, concerns predictive portability of phylogenetic-structure statistics across families. A stronger ecological claim requires a model measuring actual evolutionary dynamics or ecological drivers.
