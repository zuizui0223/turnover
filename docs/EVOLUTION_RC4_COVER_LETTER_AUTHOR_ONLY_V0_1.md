Dear Editors,

Please consider our Original Article entitled **“Repeatable lineage context and scale-dependent trait portability in plant phylogenetic memory.”**

Phylogenetic signal often informs inference beyond the lineage in which it was measured. Yet the presence of signal in multiple clades does not, by itself, establish whether its *strength* can be transferred to an unsampled clade. Our manuscript reframes that assumption as an explicit predictive question: how much lineage-wide structure recurs across functional traits, and how much trait-average information predicts an entirely withheld lineage?

We address this with 201 prospectively qualified family-by-trait systems spanning 45 vascular-plant families and 12 continuous traits, using the same phylogenetic distance–trait dissimilarity statistic across a primary tree and a backbone-native sensitivity. A separate, rule-based audit detected biologically inadmissible nonpositive measurements in five positive-only physical traits; the data-validity correction was fixed before its revised outcomes were examined and retained all 201 systems. This correction is scientifically mandatory, not a post-hoc selection for more favorable results.

Family-associated repeatability remained approximately 0.15 across raw and logarithmic representations, and the corrected raw family component exceeded a prespecified correlated-trait null (p = 0.017 and 0.013 across the two tree treatments). In contrast, the predictive value of a named trait for a fully withheld family depended on the representation: training-only shrinkage improved prediction by approximately 1.21% and 0.00% using corrected raw states, versus 5.92% and 5.51% on logarithmic states. Species-level bootstrap error accounts for approximately one quarter of typical total variation, so the large residual cannot be equated with purely biological idiosyncrasy.

The advance is not the already-established fact that phylogenetic signal differs among clades or traits. It is the *crossed predictive demonstration* that family-level repeatability and the trait-specific information that can be transported to another family are distinct questions, with the latter sensitive to how the trait state and signal statistic are defined. We avoid interpreting the distance–dissimilarity gradient as an evolutionary rate or claiming that any particular selective mechanism has been established.

To support evaluation, an anonymized manuscript contains three source-verified figures, and the derived 201-system estimates and original analysis scripts can be provided in an anonymized reproducibility package. The manuscript separates prospective primary tests from later measurement-validity and sensitivity audits. Original numerical model outputs were reproduced from the recovered source-qualified result table without changing the frozen specifications.
