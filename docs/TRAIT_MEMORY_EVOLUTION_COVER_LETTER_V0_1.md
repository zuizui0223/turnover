# Cover letter draft — Evolution

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Phylogenetic trait memory is lineage-contingent rather than trait-intrinsic across plant families,”** as an Original Article in *Evolution*.

Phylogenetic signal is commonly estimated within clades and often discussed as a property of traits. Our manuscript asks a different evolutionary question: **is the strength of phylogenetic memory for a named trait itself transferable across lineages?**

We address this using a prospectively qualified crossed dataset of 201 family × trait systems spanning 45 vascular-plant families and 12 continuous traits. The design lets us contrast two biologically distinct hypotheses on the same data structure. If memory is primarily trait-intrinsic, learning the memory strength of a trait in other families should improve prediction in a completely unseen family. If memory is lineage-contingent, family identity should instead contribute a recurring cross-trait deviation.

The resulting asymmetry is the main contribution. Trait-level portability is essentially absent after leave-one-family-out validation, even after training-only BLUP shrinkage removes most of the penalty associated with unshrunk trait means. In contrast, a family component near 15% persists after species-level sampling-error correction, geometry and provenance adjustment, trait-domain collapse, and a predeclared correlated-trait zero-family-effect null.

The robustness programme materially changed our interpretation in ways we believe strengthen the paper. We no longer describe the large original residual as biological system specificity: species-level sampling uncertainty accounts for roughly one quarter of typical total variation. We also no longer describe trait information as “anti-portable”: shrinkage shows that there is simply little transferable trait-level signal to borrow. The family component, however, remains remarkably stable across independent audits.

We do not claim that phylogenetic signal varying among traits or clades is new, nor do we present our distance-based memory gradient as a replacement for Blomberg’s K or Pagel’s lambda. The new result is predictive: **lineage context is repeatable across traits while the memory strength associated with trait identity does not meaningfully generalize to a new lineage.**

This question bears directly on a common comparative assumption—that evolutionary structure learned for a trait in one clade can inform another clade. Our results indicate that such borrowing should be validated out of lineage rather than justified by trait identity alone.

The study does not identify the biological mechanism generating the family component, and the planned all-system log-scale sensitivity remains unresolved because some systems contain nonpositive species medians under the frozen no-offset rule. We report both limitations explicitly.

Thank you for considering the manuscript.
