# Primary figure captions — truth assignability paper

## Figure 1. Failed known-truth simulations must be localized before power is interpreted

**A**, A requested simulation target must first pass a structural-feasibility audit on the realized design, then be accessible to the chosen stochastic generator, and only then can downstream recovery be evaluated. Failure at an earlier gate cannot be interpreted as estimator power. **B**, The 683 systems with stored calibration diagnostics partitioned very differently despite sharing the same target Spearman rho = 0.15. Among 407 continuous-scalar systems, 60 (14.7%) were generator no-bracket, 54 (13.3%) were successfully assigned but failed the S3 recovery gate, and 293 (72.0%) passed S3 recovery. Among 276 nominal-categorical systems, 220 (79.7%) were generator no-bracket, 38 (13.8%) were assigned but failed S3 recovery, and 18 (6.52%) passed. Thus the large representation contrast in the final endpoint is localized primarily before recovery. Panel B is the stored-diagnostics S3 subset and is distinct from the full 722-system two-geometry qualification endpoint reported in the text.

## Figure 2. A hard one-transition state-space ceiling does not explain the observed calibration ceiling

**A**, Among the 220 categorical systems that failed calibration under the latent-OU-plus-zero-threshold generator, the median maximum Spearman rho from an explicit realizable one-transition edge split was 0.773, far above the declared target rho = 0.15, whereas the median maximum rho reached on the frozen OU calibration grid was 0.0979. Every one of the 220 systems had at least one realizable edge split at or above the target. This is a one-sided ceiling witness, not proof that every discrete tree state space contains a configuration within the frozen calibration tolerance around rho = 0.15. **B**, Decomposition of the same 220 OU no-bracket systems using their stored calibration grids. In 195 systems (88.6%), the finite OU grid never reached rho = 0.15 even when low-validity points were allowed (“effect ceiling”). In 25 systems (11.4%), rho >= 0.15 was reached only after the predeclared valid-replicate fraction fell below 0.90 (“validity collapse”). For thresholded binary states, invalid replicates are monomorphic. The result rules out a hard one-transition ceiling below the benchmark as the dominant explanation and localizes the remaining failure to the generator-accessibility stage.

## Figure 3. Generator choice, state balance and recovery are separable bottlenecks

**A**, Sequential no-bracket frequency for the identical categorical target. Latent-OU thresholding produced 220/276 no-bracket systems (79.7%). Replacing only the generator with symmetric Mk2 reduced this to 170/276 (61.6%). Holding the Mk2 states and distance ranks fixed while removing the exact binary state-balance attenuation term reduced the no-bracket frequency further; four systems were held by a pre-outcome geometry-identity gate, giving a full-population bound of 42.4–43.8% without imputation. **B**, Paired transitions. Mk2 rescued 51 original OU no-bracket systems while creating one new failure among previously OU-calibrated systems. Balance normalization rescued 50 of 167 geometry-matched Mk2-rho no-bracket systems and created zero new failures among 105 matched Mk2-rho calibrated systems. **C**, Assignment and recovery remain distinct: of 106 Mk2-calibrated systems, 69 passed the frozen recovery gate and 37 (34.9%) failed recovery. Together the panels show that structural feasibility, generator accessibility, state-balance attenuation and recovery are separable components of simulation-based power inference.
