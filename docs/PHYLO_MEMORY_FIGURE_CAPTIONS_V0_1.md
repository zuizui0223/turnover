# Primary figure captions — truth assignability paper

## Figure 1. Known truth passes three distinct gates before recovery

**A**, A simulation target must first be structurally realizable by the representation on the realized design, then accessible to the chosen stochastic generator, and only then can downstream recovery be evaluated. Failure at an earlier gate cannot be interpreted as estimator power. **B**, Known-truth recovery in the frozen 722-system programme differed sharply by representation: 249/443 continuous-scalar systems (56.2%) recovered the common target Spearman rho = 0.15, compared with 6/279 nominal-categorical systems (2.15%). These rates describe the full frozen recovery gate and do not imply differences in biological conservatism.

## Figure 2. Binary state space is not the observed calibration ceiling

**A**, Among the 220 categorical systems that failed calibration under the latent-OU-plus-zero-threshold generator, the median maximum Spearman rho from an explicit realizable one-transition edge split was 0.773, far above the declared target rho = 0.15, whereas the median maximum rho reached on the frozen OU calibration grid was 0.0979. Every one of the 220 systems had at least one realizable edge split above the target. **B**, Decomposition of the same 220 OU no-bracket systems using their stored calibration grids. In 195 systems (88.6%), the finite OU grid never reached rho = 0.15 even when low-validity points were allowed (“effect ceiling”). In 25 systems (11.4%), rho >= 0.15 was reached only after the predeclared valid-replicate fraction fell below 0.90 (“validity collapse”). For thresholded binary states, invalid replicates are monomorphic. The result localizes the dominant failure to generator accessibility rather than to the binary state space itself.

## Figure 3. Generator choice, state balance and recovery are separable bottlenecks

**A**, Sequential no-bracket frequency for the identical categorical target. Latent-OU thresholding produced 220/276 no-bracket systems (79.7%). Replacing only the generator with symmetric Mk2 reduced this to 170/276 (61.6%). Holding the Mk2 states and distance ranks fixed while removing the exact binary state-balance attenuation term reduced the no-bracket frequency further; four systems were held by a pre-outcome geometry-identity gate, giving a full-population bound of 42.4–43.8% without imputation. **B**, Paired transitions. Mk2 rescued 51 original OU no-bracket systems while creating one new failure among previously OU-calibrated systems. Balance normalization rescued 50 of 167 geometry-matched Mk2-rho no-bracket systems and created zero new failures among 105 matched Mk2-rho calibrated systems. **C**, Assignment and recovery remain distinct: of 106 Mk2-calibrated systems, 69 passed the frozen recovery gate and 37 (34.9%) failed recovery. Together the panels show that structural realizability, generator accessibility, state-balance attenuation and recovery are separable components of simulation-based power inference.
