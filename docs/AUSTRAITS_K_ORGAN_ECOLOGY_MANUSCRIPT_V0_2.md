# Plant reproductive traits frequently show higher phylogenetic K, but sampled taxa shape the contrast

**Manuscript draft v0.2 — 28 October 2026**  
**Status:** Reporting-only synthesis of completed AusTraits analyses. The functional-organ grouping was selected *after* initial K outcomes; this is an exploratory comparative paper, **not an independent confirmatory experiment**. The results below are not a new analysis of the locked 201-system BIEN `rho` study.

## Abstract

Whether one plant organ is more phylogenetically conserved than another may depend both on the traits involved and the taxa available for comparison. We used twelve reproductive (seed and fruit) and vegetative (leaf, petiole and height) traits from AusTraits, comparing Blomberg's K for each trait pair on identical species and native phylogenies within families. In this **post-discovery** organ grouping, reproductive K exceeded vegetative K in **313/406 (77.1%) S3** and **314/397 (79.1%) native-prune** comparisons across **34 families**. At the family level, **27/34 S3** and **30/34 native-prune** families showed a reproductive-higher majority in their available comparisons, although notable opposing families remained. This pattern remained frequent after excluding five online-flora sources (**221/283 and 180/233** comparisons). Yet the strength of the contrast depended on which taxa remained measurable: for **the same 142 S3 family-by-trait-pair identities**, reproductive K wins changed **110→121** after restricting source-qualified species/tips, then **121→120** when only measurement-source values changed on those identical tips. The corresponding prune changes were **82→83→81** across 101 identities. We further show that the previously reported ~65% whole-family-held-out hard accuracy is algebraically determined by these original K winner counts and half-credit ties, not independent corroboration; probability-score improvements remain conditional on the post-selected grouping. Thus sampled reproductive morphology frequently has higher estimated K, but sample ascertainment materially changes its apparent regularity. We do not infer distinct evolutionary rates, selection regimes, or generality across independent primary measurement sources.

**Keywords:** AusTraits; Blomberg's K; phylogenetic signal; plant functional traits; source ascertainment; taxon sampling.

## 1. Introduction

Understanding why closely related plant species resemble one another is a central goal of comparative evolutionary ecology. The strength of resemblance depends on evolutionary processes, the phylogenetic tree, the species sampled and the trait measurements used. A statement that one trait has “more phylogenetic signal” than another is therefore incomplete without defining a common comparison population. Blomberg's K (Blomberg et al., 2003) is a covariance-based measure normalized relative to a Brownian-motion reference; it should not be interpreted as the rate of evolution, an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck attraction parameter, or direct evidence for functional constraint.

Functional organs provide one candidate level of biological prediction. Reproductive diaspores and fruits participate in dispersal and regeneration, whereas leaves, petioles and whole-plant height participate in resource acquisition and growth. Reproductive and vegetative morphologies may differ in how developmental architecture, allocation and ecology jointly shape covariation across descendants. Yet these plausible mechanisms alone do not predict the direction of K differences without comparative evidence, because both modules contain heterogeneous traits and may be measured in dissimilar taxonomic contexts. Prior studies have compared seed, leaf and height evolutionary attributes (e.g., Ackerly 2009), so a simple observation of trait-specific or organ-specific signal would not be a novel claim by itself.

A useful ecological question is whether **organ category is associated with the relative K ranking** of two plant traits compared on the same species and phylogenetic tree, and whether the apparent tendency survives changes in available observations. We also examine how its heldout-family probabilities perform under Brier scoring within the same compilation. Importantly, the coarse organ grouping was proposed after observing the initial K outcomes; a family-held-out analysis does not remove that hypothesis-selection leakage. Even a recurring organ hierarchy would not establish that reproductive traits evolve more slowly, because K also depends on which taxa and measured states enter the calculation.

We address two primary descriptive questions using an existing compilation: (1) Do reproductive traits tend to show higher Blomberg's K than vegetative traits when both organs are compared on the same species and native trees? (2) How does this tendency change when source qualification alters the available taxa and tree support, compared with changing measurement-source values while holding those selected species fixed? Family-held-out probabilistic scoring and an original-tip process-model check are secondary, post-selected diagnostics—not two more independent lines of biological validation. The organ-category hypothesis and some follow-up source controls were formulated **after** viewing original AusTraits K results; accordingly, all biological conclusions are explicitly exploratory. True external confirmation is reserved for an independently collected, prospectively qualified source, which has not yet been obtained.

## 2. Materials and methods

### 2.1 Trait classes and comparison unit

We used the **2025-06-20 AusTraits v7.0.0 release** (Falster et al., 2025, Zenodo DOI **10.5281/zenodo.15718081**), distinct from the earlier database descriptor based on version 3.0.2 (Falster et al., 2021). The family-by-trait systems followed the previously qualified K graph. The twelve source-calibratable traits were grouped into six reproductive dimensions (`fruit_height`, `fruit_length`, `fruit_width`, `seed_dry_mass`, `seed_length`, `seed_width`) and six vegetative traits (`leaf_area`, `leaf_length`, `leaf_mass_per_area`, `leaf_width`, `petiole_length`, `plant_height`). This grouping was defined **after initial empirical K rankings were inspected** and is not misrepresented as prospectively selected.

For a family-by-named-trait-pair with at least 20 common species, both trait values were evaluated on exactly the **same species** and reconstructed native phylogeny, with identical retained tips for the two organs. This comparison controls differential tip sampling *within that pair*, but not differences among pairs in the families or species they represent. The full source-qualified matched-tip comparison included 760 family-by-pair observations on the V.PhyloMaker2 S3 tree and 747 on the native-tip prune tree, spanning 42 families. The focal reproductive-versus-vegetative portion included 406 S3 comparisons across 34 families and 397 prune comparisons across the same number of families. Repeated trait pairs within one family are dependent observations, not separate evolutionary origins.

The primary descriptive response for each reproductive–vegetative pair was the sign of `K_reproductive – K_vegetative`. Absolute K magnitudes and relative rankings are not equated with selection coefficients or evolutionary rate.

### 2.2 Original observation handling, transformations, native trees and K calculation

The matched-tip computation followed the existing source-native AusTraits processing code (`audit_austraits_k_shared_taxon_pair_support_v0_1.py`, `run_austraits_k_shared_taxon_pairs_batch_v0_1.py` and `fit_austraits_k_shared_taxon_pairs_v0_1.R`). Only records identified at **species taxonomic rank**, with nonmissing genus, binomial, original DatasetID and ObservationID, were eligible. The numeric value had to be finite and its recorded unit had to **exactly match the configured source-native unit for that trait**. Identical system–genus–species–dataset–observation–value records were deduplicated. For each family-by-trait system, the median of eligible numeric observations for each species formed its species state; **median values of zero or below were excluded**. Consequently, these K comparisons concern strictly positive, source-normalized species medians—not untransformed specimen measurements or within-species variance.

For a pair, we intersected the two positive-median species lists, required consistent genus identity and at least **20 common species**, and then transformed both retained species-state vectors using the **natural logarithm**. The identical selected species were used for both organs in each within-family K contrast. Pair-specific shared-species intersections varied among trait pairs; this design does **not** imply that all twelve traits were observed on one global common-species cohort.

The native phylogenies were built using `V.PhyloMaker2::phylo.maker` (Jin & Qian, 2022) with `GBOTB.extended.TPL` and `nodes.info.1.TPL` under scenario **S3**. The separate native-prune treatment retained only the relevant taxa assigned the original prune status and pruned the GBOTB backbone; it did not impute missing taxa to force support. Trees or comparisons were held out if fewer than 20 native tips remained, tree branch lengths were invalid, or either logged trait had zero variance. Branch lengths were checked for finiteness and nonnegativity. We estimated Blomberg's K for each **logged** species-median vector using the frozen real-tree kernel, with numerical parity verified against `phytools::phylosig(..., method = "K", test = FALSE)` (Revell, 2012) on supported reference trees. The key comparison is therefore the difference in estimated K **on the same native tree and same logged species states**; the `log(K_reproductive) – log(K_vegetative)` scale was used for sensitivity magnitudes where both K values were positive.

### 2.3 Predicting a previously unseen plant family

For each held-out family, the prediction model learned coefficients only from family-by-trait-pair signs in **other families in the same AusTraits compilation**. Importantly, the functional-organ grouping was selected after the original empirical K outcomes across this compilation were known. The predictive analysis therefore has **non-nested hypothesis selection**: it blocks family-specific model fitting, but it does not independently re-select the organ grouping within each training fold. Consequently, the accuracy and Brier confidence intervals are retrospective conditional performance, not an unbiased estimate of the prospective performance of a grouping chosen blind to every test family. The organ-only classifier used a single signed reproductive-versus-vegetative contrast, with no intercept and the previously fixed ridge penalty; pooled sign, separately estimated twelve named-trait scores, and same-named-pair priors provided comparison predictors. Scoring includes all 760/747 supported pair comparisons; within-organ comparisons have an organ contrast of zero and are scored as ties rather than artificially assigned to the reproductive class.

We report hard-sign accuracy and Brier probability loss; family-cluster bootstrap intervals characterize differences from the pooled sign prior. Crucially, hard accuracy is *not an additional independent outcome*: because the organ model has no intercept and gives exactly 0.5 predicted probability for same-organ pairs, those pairs always contribute 0.5 accuracy credit. All family-held-out fits retain a positive organ coefficient: the observed reproductive-minus-vegetative win margins are 220 S3 and 231 prune, whereas any held-out family contains at most 36 cross-organ trait-pair rows (six reproductive × six vegetative traits), leaving positive training margins of at least 184 and 195. The sign of the one-parameter ridge logistic coefficient must therefore remain positive in every fold. Consequently, the full-cohort model's **hard accuracy is fixed by the already-observed cross-organ winner count**. With `W` denoting reproductive higher-K pairs, `N_all` all supported pair rows, and `N_cross` the cross-organ rows, the original scoring rule gives:

**Organ-only hard accuracy = (W + 0.5 × (N_all − N_cross)) / N_all.**

**S3:** (313 + 0.5 × 354) / 760 = 490/760 = **64.47%**. **Native prune:** (314 + 0.5 × 350) / 747 = 489/747 = **65.46%**.

The coefficients' **probability magnitudes**, and hence heldout-family Brier losses, are not determined by that binary identity; they evaluate conditional retrospective probabilistic prediction scores, not independently selected functional modules or a separately demonstrated calibration property. Leaving a family out does **not** leave out historical source publications or shared protocols, so this remains within-compilation post-selected prediction. The exact count identity is checked in `scripts/qa/audit_austraits_organ_lofo_accuracy_identity_v0_1.py` without fitting any model or reopening traits.

### 2.4 Separating source and taxon-ascertainment sensitivity

Three completed, outcome-labelled exploratory robustness analyses addressed different levels of sample construction:

1. After excluding five large electronic flora contributors, we compared traits on common species/trees with medians from the remaining original AusTraits contributing datasets.
2. We selected pairs with at least 20 species for which both organs had source records from an identical remaining non-flora AusTraits DatasetID. This source identity is not proof of the same directly measured physical specimen.
3. On the **same 142 S3 (101 prune) selected family-by-trait-pair identities**, we compared (A) original broader non-flora species/tip supports and all remaining measurements, (B) the more restrictive common-DatasetID species/tip supports but all remaining measurements, and (C) those exact narrower species/tips/trees using only the shared-source measurements.

A versus B changes the sampled species, the reconstructed native tree geometry and K normalization together. B versus C holds taxa/tree fixed and changes only the contributing values. Descriptive sign-transition counts were reconstructed algebraically from already archived totals and flip counts, not from additional trait outcome searches. For family-level uncertainty, the source analyses used 5,000 whole-family bootstrap draws rather than treating overlapping pair rows as independent.

### 2.5 Evolutionary-process model adequacy is a separate estimand

A previously fitted homogeneous OU model with source-calibrated observational heterogeneity reproduced the *predeclared scalar magnitude of rank portability*, so the original null classification remains `RANK_PORTABILITY_NOT_BEYOND_ALL_SOURCE_OU_ENVELOPES`. A later, **post-discovery** posterior-predictive check asked whether tested members of that model family reproduce the *particular reproductive-versus-vegetative direction*. It was evaluated only on the **original, nonmatched family-by-trait systems** (410 cross-organ comparisons) to match the geometry of the original simulations. It is **not a calibrated null for the 406/397 matched-tip organ comparisons**, nor a preregistered p-value for a hypothesis selected from those outcomes.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 A consistent but not universal organ-level K pattern

Reproductive morphology showed greater K in **313/406 (77.09%) S3** and **314/397 (79.09%) prune** common-tip cross-organ comparisons among 34 families. Family-equal mean reproductive-higher proportions were **74.7% S3** (95% whole-family bootstrap CI **64.3–84.4%**) and **75.8% prune** (67.4–84.1%). The descriptive trend was not explained solely by the source-dense seed-dry-mass trait: after excluding its comparisons, reproductive K remained higher in **263/344 (76.5%)** and **268/337 (79.5%)** comparisons, respectively. For fruit traits alone versus vegetative traits, the rates were **149/193 (77.2%)** and **150/189 (79.4%)**.

Nevertheless, the direction is not uniform across families. In the frozen original per-family aggregates, **27/34 S3** and **30/34 native-prune** families showed a reproductive-higher majority of their observed cross-organ comparisons; these are family-level summaries, not 34 independent evolutionary origins. Removing any single family left the *pair-weighted* reproductive-higher rate between **75.1–79.1% S3** and **77.1–80.8% prune**, so the pooled contrast was not due to one heavily represented family. Important descriptive exceptions remained: **Asteraceae** was near parity (**16/30 S3; 16/27 prune**), whereas **Casuarinaceae** (**2/12; 3/12**) and **Rubiaceae** (**1/6; 2/6**) favored vegetative K on both trees. **Elaeocarpaceae, Lauraceae and Phyllanthaceae** changed the direction of their family-level majority between the two related tree treatments. These named-family checks were inspected after the original outcomes and are *not* independently validated family-specific evolutionary mechanisms; shared taxa/traits/sources and organ homology may explain some differences (Supplementary Table S1 and source-pinned family audit).

### 3.2 The tendency survives major source restrictions

When five dominant electronic floras were excluded, reproductive K remained higher in **221/283 (78.1%) S3** comparisons across 29 families and **180/233 (77.3%) prune** comparisons across 25 families. Under the more stringent common remaining non-flora DatasetID rule, the result was **120/142 (84.5%) S3** across 24 families and **81/101 (80.2%) prune** across 18 families. The strict cohort's equal-family rates were **82.2%** (95% family-bootstrap 71.1–91.4%) and **69.9%** (53.1–85.0%). These are internal sensitivity results using the same original AusTraits source universe, not independent empirical replications.

### 3.3 Taxon selection changes apparent organ superiority more than value substitution in the S3 comparison

Comparing the **same exact 142 S3 pair identities**, reproductive-higher cases changed from **110/142** on broad non-flora tips to **121/142** on the common-source-qualified narrower tips. There were **23 sign reversals**: algebraically, **17** went from vegetative-higher to reproductive-higher and **6** went in the opposite direction. Switching to only shared-source observations while holding these narrower tips/trees constant changed **121/142 to 120/142**, with **9 reversals** (**4** vegetative→reproductive; **5** reproductive→vegetative).

The prune-only counterpart used the same **101 pair identities** within that tree treatment: **82→83→81** reproductive-higher cases. The first transition comprised **7** vegetative→reproductive and **6** reproductive→vegetative changes (13 reversals); the second comprised **5** and **7**, respectively (12 reversals). The median absolute shifts in the within-pair log-K contrast were **0.1964 then 0.0758** on S3 and **0.2513 then 0.0948** on prune.

These exact sign flows resolve an important ambiguity: the seemingly stronger **84.5%** S3 advantage in the strict subset arises substantially when the available **species and tips** change, not when identical tips are given source-restricted numerical medians. The conclusion differs in strength by tree treatment, and the first transition also changes tree pruning and K scaling. We therefore do **not** interpret those shifts causally as evolutionary responses to source choice.

### 3.4 Held-out-family binary accuracy contains no second organ signal; Brier loss measures conditional predictive performance

The organ-only model's nominal heldout-family hard accuracies of **64.47% S3** and **65.46% prune** are **exactly** \((313+354/2)/760=490/760\) and \((314+350/2)/747=489/747\), respectively. In both cases, the first term counts the same already-observed reproductive-higher comparisons in Section 3.1; the second is fixed half-credit from within-organ comparisons, which always have model probability 0.5. Because every fold's logistic organ coefficient has the same positive sign, leaving out a family cannot change these hard-label predictions. Thus the 65% and 77–79% descriptions are **not two independent empirical successes**. The comparison with pooled hard-sign accuracy (**52.63% S3**, **53.82% prune**) does not create an independent new sample or a second mechanism test.

Probabilistic prediction performance is a distinct descriptive output: the organ-model Brier loss improved from **0.25062 to 0.21378** on S3 and **0.25017 to 0.20671** on prune relative to the pooled prior. Original whole-family bootstrap 95% intervals for the Brier improvement were **+0.00433 to +0.06388** and **+0.01946 to +0.06338**. A more flexible named-trait model had losses **0.21792 S3** and **0.21156 prune**; a reliable direct head-to-head advantage of the one-coefficient organ model over this alternative was not established. Importantly, **the organ grouping was selected after initial results from the same families**, so these family-held-out Brier gains remain *conditional, non-nested* within-AusTraits evidence, not unbiased prospective transfer to independent lineages and sources.

### 3.5 A posterior predictive discrepancy does not identify the evolutionary mechanism

On the separate original unequal-tip systems, reproductive K was higher in **302/410 (73.7%) S3** and **330/410 (80.5%) prune** comparisons. Tested homogeneous OU-plus-source-observation models underpredicted this particular post-selected organ direction across their specified cases, even though they matched the different predeclared scalar rank-portability statistic. The apparent mismatch is therefore **a descriptive model-adequacy signal for a post-selected feature**, not a preregistered rejection of homogeneous evolution, nor evidence that organ-specific OU attraction rates or stabilizing selection have been identified.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 An organ-level tendency, not two independent predictive discoveries

The ecological pattern is that selected reproductive seed/fruit morphology more often exhibits higher estimated K than vegetative morphology on common phylogenetic tips. A simple functional-category label summarizes part of this within-AusTraits tendency, but does **not** establish a transferable evolutionary law. Our exact accuracy audit further shows that the frequently cited 64.5–65.5% LOFO hard accuracy adds **no independent directional evidence** beyond the 77–79% cross-organ winners. Its lower denominator-normalized percentage is caused by scoring within-organ comparisons as half-credit ties. This is an important distinction for biological interpretation: an apparently separate prediction result can simply restate the same original sample's exploratory regularity in a different metric.

Original conditional Brier improvements still reflect how coefficient magnitudes estimated from other families determine probabilities scored under a **post-selected** grouping. Brier loss is a proper prediction score; improvement does not by itself demonstrate calibration or separate calibration from resolution. They do not remove module-selection leakage, study-source reuse or deeper phylogenetic dependence among families. The substantive contribution of this paper must therefore rely on the **organ-associated pattern itself and the sensitivity of its apparent strength to which taxa and sources survive**, rather than presenting hard-label accuracy as a second confirmation.

### 4.2 Sampling determines what looks conserved

The paired three-stage analysis reveals that a more stringent source requirement can simultaneously improve citation consistency and change which species remain available. That latter change can alter the direction of comparative phylogenetic K more than switching the source measurements on otherwise identical species/trees. This is substantive for comparative biology: choosing a “better controlled” subset can accidentally strengthen the appearance of an evolutionary hierarchy if the improved source qualification selects a different slice of the lineage's phenotypic diversity.

The effect cannot automatically be corrected by subtracting “source noise”, because the selection stage changes the underlying tree-tip graph, not merely independent measurement precision. Observational studies should therefore report source qualification *and* within-identity taxon-support transitions, ideally with primary specimen-level measures.

**Source contribution audit (descriptive, no new K fits).** Reconstructing the original public AusTraits v7.0.0 source records showed **190 DatasetIDs** in the 760 matched-tip K source pool and **44 DatasetIDs** contributing to the strict 142-pair common-DatasetID source population. Within the latter's **31,401 deduplicated measurement rows**, **23,122 (73.6%)** came from five DatasetIDs (Kooyman_2011, Maslin_2012, Cooper_2013, GrassBase_2014 and Everingham_2020). These rows are **not independent specimens, primary publications, evolutionary events or K comparisons**. Original methods metadata may contain secondary/original-source credits or multiple source_id values; a shared DatasetID cannot establish independent original studies or common specimens. Such source dependence is not represented by conditional whole-family bootstrap intervals. This source-credit inventory does not estimate a new K effect; [original-version citation and metadata review](https://github.com/zuizui0223/turnover/tree/paper/austraits-organ-ecology-v0-2/results/austraits_organ_paper_compact_source_credit_review_v0_1) separates original primary, secondary and source-original keys for human review.

### 4.3 What mechanisms remain plausible?

Organ developmental integration, allocation, trait covariance, variation in selection and evolutionary history are possible explanations for the reproductive K pattern. However, they cannot be identified from K sign alone. Source-specific taxon composition, unequal original measurement precision, leaf-versus-leaflet ambiguity and structures such as nutlets, samaras or infructescences also remain viable explanations. The tested homogeneous OU-plus-source model underpredicted the *particular selected direction* on original unmatched tips, but realistic cross-trait covariance and source-specific sampling dependencies were not completely represented. No claim of stronger stabilizing selection, canalization or a slower rate of reproductive evolution is warranted.

### 4.4 Scope and next evidence

This result applies to the qualified continuous morphology and source composition of AusTraits; it is not a global representative survey of every vascular-plant family. The grouping was defined after reviewing original outcomes, so the family-bootstrap confidence intervals quantify conditional variation given a selected grouping, not post-selection discovery uncertainty. The organ grouping and sensitivity studies are post-outcome, source regimes overlap across held-out families and the two phylogenetic treatments share parts of the underlying tree information. Independent evidence requires primary individual- or specimen-level measurements of both organs on matched species, harmonized organ homology, original-reference disjointness from AusTraits and supported phylogenies across at least twelve independent families. Source qualification for those criteria has **not passed**. This manuscript should therefore be presented and reviewed as a carefully controlled exploratory discovery with specific prospective predictions, not as completed independent ecological confirmation.

### Research ethics and statistical scope

No experimental treatment, new biological observation, prospective model selection or new biological hypothesis fit was performed for this reporting revision. Original K calculations were re-executed strictly as numerical reproducibility checks on unchanged original data and phylogenies. Family-block bootstrap intervals describe conditional variability among the available families; deep phylogenetic relatedness and correlated underlying literature sources can create dependence *between* family clusters, so these intervals are **not** full evolutionary-process or post-selection uncertainty intervals.
