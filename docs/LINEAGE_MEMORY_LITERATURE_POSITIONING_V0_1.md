# Literature positioning — lineage repeatability of phylogenetic trait memory

Status: drafted before the aggregate real-data repeatability result is opened.

## Nearest traditions

### Trait-specific phylogenetic signal

The dominant comparative tradition asks whether a given trait shows phylogenetic signal, using statistics such as Blomberg's K, Pagel's lambda, Moran-type autocorrelation, or related measures. Münkemüller et al. (2012, Methods in Ecology and Evolution, doi:10.1111/j.2041-210X.2012.00196.x) emphasize that phylogenetic signal is a pattern and that different signal statistics need not have a simple one-to-one process interpretation.

The present study does not replace those statistics with another global signal index. Its response is a within-family distance-decay statistic, estimated in the same way for each family × trait system.

### Comparing evolutionary tempo among traits and clades

Ackerly (2009, PNAS, doi:10.1073/pnas.0901635106) directly compared evolutionary rates for plant height, leaf size, and seed size across four to six woody clades and showed strong among-clade heterogeneity in the rate of functional-trait evolution.

This is the closest conceptual precedent: evolutionary conservatism can depend strongly on lineage context rather than being an invariant property of a trait.

The new design changes the scale of the comparison. Instead of a small set of clade-specific rate estimates considered one trait at a time, it fixes a connected crossed matrix of 45 plant families × 12 traits and asks whether **family identity itself is repeatable across traits after trait-specific mean memory loss has been removed**.

### Broad phylogenetic variance partitioning

Other studies partition trait variation among phylogenetic/taxonomic levels or estimate global phylogenetic effects for one or several traits. These address whether trait values carry evolutionary structure.

The present parameter is different: it is the repeatability of the **rate at which similarity decays with evolutionary separation**, not variance in the trait value itself.

## Specific gap

The study is designed around a crossed question that standard phylogenetic-signal analyses do not directly answer:

> If two traits have different average evolutionary memory, do the same plant families nevertheless recur as unusually conservative or unusually labile across both traits?

A family-level random intercept after trait fixed effects is a direct estimate of that recurring lineage regime.

## Primary novelty claim if the gate/result supports it

A defensible novelty statement is:

> We separate trait-specific mean evolutionary memory from a lineage-level component that is replicated across different traits, using a prospectively qualified crossed family × trait design.

Do not claim that this is the first study of clade heterogeneity in trait evolution; Ackerly (2009) and related comparative studies already establish that broad concept.

Do not claim that lineage effects dominate trait effects; that separate variance-ratio question was prospectively closed before real effects were opened.
